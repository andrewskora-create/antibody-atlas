Built on the work of the scientific community

Atlas integrates methods developed by the research groups credited below. Their models, algorithms and reference resources remain their work. Atlas adds the local interface, workflow integration, evidence joins, comparison displays and explicitly labeled screening heuristics. Please cite each underlying method you use; citing Atlas alone does not credit its contributors.

Sources are listed for transparency, including optional methods and contextual literature. A citation does not mean that a module ran, that its authors endorse Atlas, or that a paper validates Atlas’s combined screening rules. Per-result availability and provenance determine what actually ran.

AntiBERTy

Local antibody language-model species/chain classification and mean masked-residue pseudo log-likelihood when explicitly run.

Deciphering antibody affinity maturation with language models and weakly supervised learning (Ruffolo, Gray and Sulam, 2021; preprint)
Original software / resource

Sequence-model likelihood and species classification are not percent human framework identity, immunogenicity, affinity, or clinical developability probabilities. AntiBERTy is distinct from AntiBERTa.

ImmuneBuilder / ABodyBuilder2

Local paired antibody variable-domain structure prediction; ImmuneBuilder also provides NanoBodyBuilder2 for heavy-only domains.

ImmuneBuilder: Deep-Learning models for predicting the structures of immune proteins (Abanades et al., 2023)
Original software / resource

Predicted coordinates and ensemble uncertainty are not experimental structures. Loop/side-chain errors and conformational changes affect surface and PTM screens; Fv predictions do not establish full-IgG behavior.

ANARCII

Local antigen-receptor numbering used to assign the selected IMGT or Kabat positions and regions.

ANARCII: A Generalised Language Model for Antigen Receptor Numbering (Greenshields-Watson et al., 2025; preprint)
Original software / resource

Numbering conventions define different CDR boundaries. Numbering does not identify an experimentally established antigen-contacting paratope; ambiguous/unassigned residues require review.

FreeSASA

Local solvent-accessible surface area, including relative side-chain exposure for PTM screening and local patch descriptors.

FreeSASA: An open source C library for solvent accessible surface area calculation (Mitternacht, 2016)
Original software / resource

SASA is geometric exposure in the supplied structure. It is not percent modified or a PTM probability; relative side-chain, whole-residue and atom-specific SASA are different quantities.

OGRDB human germline reference

Reference human V alleles for the app's exact framework identity calculation; the result records the bundled locus release and reference hash.

OGRDB: a reference database of inferred immune receptor genes (Lees et al., 2020)
Original software / resource

The app's FR1–FR3 identity metric is its own calculation against this reference, not a validated clinical ADA or immunogenicity prediction. Allele choice and denominator affect the percentage; CDRs and FR4 are excluded.

NGL Viewer

Local browser rendering of coordinates, surfaces, side chains, heat maps and PTM markers.

NGL viewer: web-based molecular graphics for large complexes (Rose et al., 2018)
Original software / resource

Rendering does not validate the model. Patch heat colors and PTM marker colors are assigned by this app, not an NGL scientific risk predictor.

Therapeutic Antibody Profiler (official TAP)

Optional external Oxford service; the local app provides a handoff rather than executing official TAP.

Five computational developability guidelines for therapeutic antibody profiling (Raybould et al., 2019)
Original software / resource

Local exposed-cluster counts are not TAP PSH, PPC, PNC or SFvCSP scores. Official submission sends the pasted sequence to Oxford. Reference-distribution flags do not guarantee drug success.

PROPERMAB

Official local sequence and structural descriptor calculations when explicitly run; per-run results identify completed and unavailable features.

PROPERMAB: an integrative framework for in silico prediction of antibody developability using machine learning (Li et al., 2025)
Original software / resource

The separate Atlas contact-graph display is not PROPERMAB. Local descriptor output does not constitute a fitted druggability model; no trained outcome checkpoint is supplied. Academic research license applies. Per-run availability identifies the APBS/NanoShaper scope.

App PTM screening rules

App-authored sequence motifs plus relative side-chain exposure, with separate potential CDR functional impact and physical-residue deduplication.

Yang et al. 2017: Met oxidation and side-chain SASA
Sharma et al. 2014: MD-based Trp oxidation and Asp isomerization
Yan et al. 2018: structure-based Asn deamidation
Hoffmann et al. 2024: deamidation/isomerization model comparison
N-glycosylation and protein folding (2017)

The 10%/30% exposure bins and high/review/lower categories are disclosed screening heuristics, not calibrated probabilities, measured PTM levels or literature-validated universal cutoffs. Supporting literature does not validate this combined rule set.

Biopython ProtParam and Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy

Local sequence molecular mass, isoelectric point, aromatic fraction and GRAVY calculations; the app uses the Kyte-Doolittle scale for its sliding-window display.

A simple method for displaying the hydropathic character of a protein (Kyte and Doolittle, 1982)
Original software / resource

These are sequence descriptors. The app-authored 7-residue hydrophobic-window threshold and simple side-chain pKa charge model do not predict measured aggregation, viscosity, solubility, or PK.

Therapeutic PTM evidence and clinical sequence retention

Local sequence-specific comparison with literature-documented PTMs and a separate Phase III/approved therapeutic sequence snapshot.

Thera-SAbDab: the Therapeutic Structural Antibody Database (Raybould et al., 2020)
Original software / resource

Clinical sequence retention does not establish modification, tolerance, current regulatory status, or engineering history. Source stage snapshot is February 2025. Human V-gene CDR identity is a protein-sequence match, not proven germline ancestry. Each experimental observation has its own citation and conditions.

Additional chemical and cysteine flags

Asp-Pro cleavage, conditional N-terminal Q/E cyclization, and cysteines outside canonical IMGT Cys23/Cys104.

Detecting aspartate isomerization and backbone cleavage by NMR (2021)
N-terminal Glu to pyroglutamate conversion in vivo (2011)
IMGT conserved numbering: Cys23 and Cys104
Noncanonical intradomain disulfide linkages can stabilize variable domains (2023)

Motif presence is not a reaction rate. Asp-Pro and noncanonical cysteines receive review priority independent of SASA. Additional cysteines may be stabilizing disulfides, not free thiols. Pyroglutamate requires a free mature terminus and is lower-priority characterization context, not generic adverse function.

Paragraph

Local paratope prediction over the predicted Fv, used to report predicted antigen contact per position and to prefer PTM fixes away from the binding surface.

Paragraph: antibody paratope prediction using graph neural networks with minimal feature vectors (Chinery, Wahome, Moal and Deane, 2023)
Original software / resource

Predicted paratope, not a measured epitope. The model does not know this antibody’s antigen and scores only IMGT CDR+/-2 positions, so framework contacts cannot be predicted. The 0.734 classifier cut-off is the authors’ recommendation for recovering the observed number of binding residues, not an accuracy optimum. A local validation against a held-out therapeutic antibody cocrystal is in PTM_FIX_ENGINEERING.md.

Conservative substitution ranking

Ranking of candidate PTM fixes by BLOSUM62 exchange score, a physicochemical distance, human germline residue frequency and clinical-stage residue frequency at the aligned position.

Amino acid substitution matrices from protein blocks (Henikoff and Henikoff, 1992)
OGRDB human germline reference (Lees et al., 2020)
Thera-SAbDab clinical-stage sequences (Raybould et al., 2020)

Conservativeness and positional precedent are not predictions of retained binding, potency, expression or stability. Germline and clinical frequencies are observational: a common residue is not established as tolerated in this antibody, and an absent one is not established as harmful.

Charge, surface and pharmacokinetic context

Interpretation of net charge, pI and charge-patch descriptors in relation to nonspecific binding, clearance and high-concentration viscosity.

In silico selection of therapeutic antibodies for development: viscosity, clearance, and chemical stability (Sharma et al., 2014)
Datta-Mannan et al. 2016 — surface charge engineering, self-association and clearance
Rai et al. 2023 — electrostatic surface and antibody viscosity
Jain et al. 2017 — biophysical properties of clinical-stage antibodies

The charge–clearance relationship is non-monotonic: both strongly positive and strongly negative variants have shown faster clearance, so a low pI is not automatically favourable. These descriptors inform clearance and viscosity risk; none of them predicts it, and viscosity is concentration-dependent and must be measured.

Human clearance against estimated pI and poly-specificity

Direction and interpretation of the pharmacokinetic charge flag, using the published association between estimated isoelectric point and measured linear clearance in clinical-stage antibodies.

Identifying biophysical assays and properties that enrich for slow clearance in clinical-stage therapeutic antibodies (Grinshpun et al., 2021)

The published analysis used 64 full-length IgG molecules and measured human linear clearance. This project computes pI from variable domains only, so the value is a rank-order proxy and not the molecule pI the authors thresholded on. The authors also identify a poly-specificity assay as the stronger discriminator, and that is an experiment this project cannot substitute for. Clearance is additionally governed by FcRn interaction, target-mediated disposition and anti-drug antibodies, none of which is visible from a variable-domain sequence. No clearance value is predicted.

Clinical-stage biophysical property boundaries

Context for whether a descriptor sits inside the range spanned by antibodies that reached advanced clinical stages.

Biophysical properties of the clinical-stage antibody landscape (Jain et al., 2017)

The published boundaries come from twelve measured assays on 137 isotype-matched full-length IgG1 molecules. This project has not measured any of those assays and does not reproduce their thresholds; the reference is cited for the concept of empirical clinical-stage limits, not as a validation of any value computed here.

Formulation pH margin from the isoelectric point

Basis for the colloidal-stability flag: net charge approaches zero near the isoelectric point, reducing electrostatic repulsion between molecules.

Physical stability of proteins in aqueous solution: mechanism and driving forces in nonnative protein aggregation (Chi et al., 2003)

The pH-versus-pI margin is a colloidal-stability heuristic, not an aggregation prediction. Real behaviour depends on ionic strength, buffer species, excipients, concentration and conformational stability, none of which is modelled. The margin is computed from a variable-domain pI, which is not the full molecule pI. This project sets the margin bands as its own screening policy.

Human immunoglobulin constant regions (UniProtKB)

Secreted heavy (IGHG1-4) and light (IGKC, IGLC1/2/3/7) constant-region sequences, appended to the variable domains so molecule-level charge descriptors are computed on a full-length H2L2 rather than on the Fv alone.

UniProt: the Universal Protein Knowledgebase
Original software / resource

Sequences are the UniProt canonical secreted isoform for one allotype per subclass. Allotypes differ at charged positions and shift the computed pI. No glycan is modelled, so sialylated glycoforms that lower the real pI are absent, and no therapeutic Fc engineering (IgG4 S228P, silencing, half-life or charge variants) is applied. Assembly is sequence concatenation, not a structural model. UniProtKB data are CC BY 4.0.

DeepSP

Sequence-only prediction of spatial aggregation propensity (SAP_pos) and spatial charge maps (SCM_neg, SCM_pos) over the CDRs and the whole Fv. Used as a fast, MIT-licensed alternative to the structure-based descriptor route, which needs folding and, for the PROPERMAB descriptors, a Regeneron academic licence.

DeepSP: Deep learning-based spatial properties to predict monoclonal antibody stability (Kalejaye, Wu, Terry and Lai, 2024)
Original software / resource

DeepSP predicts what a molecular-dynamics simulation would have reported, not what an experiment measured, so it carries the simulation protocol's error as well as its own regression error. It was trained on natural human Fv pairs from the Observed Antibody Space, so the training distribution is a natural repertoire rather than a clinical-stage panel. Its alignment grid holds at most seventeen CDR-H3 insertions and no light-chain insertions; a molecule that overflows it is reported as unreliable rather than scored. Weights are the authors' own, converted from Keras to numpy and verified against the sixteen reference molecules they publish with the model.

ABodyBuilder2 model ensemble

Folding the same Fv with several of ABodyBuilder2's four trained models and taking the worst-case relative side-chain exposure across them, so a PTM site that is exposed in any model is treated as exposed.

ImmuneBuilder: Deep-Learning models for predicting the structures of immune proteins (Abanades et al., 2023)
Original software / resource

Agreement between models is not accuracy. The four models share training data and can agree while all being wrong, so a stable exposure band is not experimental evidence. Coordinates are never averaged: each model is analysed separately and only the per-residue exposure is reconciled. The flag-if-any rule raises sensitivity at the cost of specificity by design, which means more sites to check, not more real liabilities. Checkpoints are verified before use and a corrupt one is reported rather than skipped.

Side-chain repacking (PDBFixer + OpenMM)

Builds the substituted side chain, relaxes it and its immediate neighbours against a rigid environment, and reports the hydrogen bonds lost and gained and any steric clash introduced. Answers what the molecule looks like after a substitution, rather than inferring the consequence by removing the wild-type side chain.

OpenMM 7: Rapid development of high performance algorithms for molecular dynamics (Eastman et al., 2017)
Original software / resource

No free energy is reported and none should be inferred: force-field potential energies of two different sequences are not comparable, so a kcal/mol here would be dominated by chemistry unrelated to stability. Hydrogen-bond counts and clashes are what this measures. No solvent model is used, which is affordable only because the environment is held rigid; the mutated side chain relaxes into a fixed pocket rather than the structure re-equilibrating. A single relaxed conformation is not a rotamer search, so a substitution that would be tolerated in a different rotamer can read as a loss. On a predicted backbone this models one thing on top of another, and the result carries its structure source for that reason. Off-site bond changes are reported as protocol noise so the signal-to-noise of each answer is visible.

ThermoMPNN — Kuhlman Lab and contributors

Local learned single-substitution folding ΔΔG estimate from the parent chain; separate from OpenMM relaxation.

Transfer learning to leverage larger datasets for improved prediction of protein stability changes (2024)
Original software / resource

Single-chain learned prediction, not experimental or antigen-binding free energy. ProteinMPNN, GraphProteinDesign and LightAttention notices are retained by the installer.
